# Ballina Adventure Park — Paintball Waiver (v2)

A rewritten paintball liability waiver for NSW, designed to fit on a **single A4 page**
and to maximise enforceability under current NSW and federal law.

## Files
- `Ballina-Adventure-Park-Paintball-Waiver.html` — print-ready source (open in any browser → Print → Save as PDF, or print directly).
- `Ballina-Adventure-Park-Paintball-Waiver.pdf` — rendered single-page A4 version.
- `Ballina-Adventure-Park-Paintball-Waiver.md` — plain text to paste into Google Docs / Word.

## Why it is structured this way (legal basis)
A NSW recreational-activity waiver only works if it stacks **four** independent protections,
because no single one covers everything:

1. **Risk warning — Civil Liability Act 2002 (NSW) s 5M.** A clear written warning of the
   *general nature* of the risks removes the duty of care for that recreational activity. For
   under-18s the warning must reach a parent/guardian — handled by the guardian section.
2. **Obvious risk of a dangerous recreational activity — s 5L.** Paintball is very likely a
   "dangerous recreational activity"; there is no liability for harm from the materialisation
   of an *obvious risk* of such an activity. Section 2 spells the risks out so they are obvious.
3. **Waiver of contractual duty of care — s 5N.** An express contract term excluding/restricting
   liability for breach of the warranty to use reasonable care and skill is enforceable in NSW.
4. **Exclusion of consumer guarantees — Competition & Consumer Act 2010 (Cth) s 139A.** Lets a
   recreational-services supplier exclude/restrict/modify the ACL consumer guarantees.

## Important limits (deliberately reflected in the wording)
- **You cannot exclude liability for death or significant personal injury caused by the
  *reckless conduct* of the supplier** (s 139A(4)–(6)). The form says so. Trying to exclude it
  anyway can make a clause look overreaching; acknowledging it makes the rest more robust.
- **Waivers must be clear, not vague.** NSW courts strike down "vague and meaningless" waivers
  (*Belna Pty Ltd v Irwin* [2009] NSWCA 46). The wording here is plain and conspicuous.
- **Minors cannot be bound by an indemnity they sign.** The guardian therefore indemnifies and
  accepts the risk warning **both for the child and in their own right**.

## What was removed/changed from v1
- Dropped the home address line (not needed — kept name, DOB, one phone, email, emergency contact).
- Added the statutory **risk warning** and **ACL warning** boxes (the parts that actually create
  the protection — v1 lacked these).
- Added a **privacy notice** (APP 5 collection notice).
- Strengthened the **minor consent** so the guardian is personally bound.
- Added the **reckless-conduct carve-out** and a **severance** clause.
- Spelled out the **natural bushland / uneven-terrain and native-wildlife hazards** so they count as
  "obvious risks" under s 5L (the leading NSW paintball case, *Action Paintball Games Pty Ltd v Barker*
  [2013] NSWCA 128, also concerned a natural-obstacle field).
- Added a short **medical declaration**, a **guardian phone** field, and an undertaking that the participant
  **will complete the video safety induction** before playing (mandatory mask wearing + safety briefing —
  tracked on the separate induction form). Worded as a forward undertaking because the form is signed
  *before* the video, and so the waiver works as a standalone document.

## Privacy: do you need a separate Privacy Policy?
The form's privacy paragraph is a **self-contained collection notice** (APP 5) plus **consent** to collect the
health/sensitive info in the medical line — it does **not** rely on a separate Privacy Policy document.

Whether you're legally required to have a full Privacy Policy:
- The **Privacy Act 1988 (Cth)** generally only binds businesses with **annual turnover over $3 million**.
  As at June 2026 that **small-business exemption still applies**, so a sub-$3M paintball operator is likely
  **not legally required** to have a Privacy Policy *yet*.
- Collecting a fitness/medical declaration is unlikely to make you a "health service provider" (the exception
  that strips the exemption), so that trigger probably doesn't bite — but you are collecting **sensitive
  (health) information**, so getting **consent** (now built into the form) and only collecting what you need
  is best practice regardless.
- **This is changing:** the Government's Tranche 2 privacy reforms are expected to **remove the small-business
  exemption in 2026–2027**. When that lands, you'll likely need a public Privacy Policy. Worth putting a simple
  one on your website ahead of time; it's cheap insurance and good for customer trust.

## ⚠️ Not legal advice
This is a researched template, not legal advice. Have it reviewed and signed off by an
Australian solicitor (ideally one who does recreational-activity / public liability work)
before relying on it, and confirm it matches the requirements of your **public liability
insurance policy** — insurers often mandate specific waiver wording.
