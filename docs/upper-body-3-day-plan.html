Upper-Body Hypertrophy, 3 Days a Week

Arms-priority version. Full-body layout with an upper-body bias. Chest, back, and arms are trained every session, with arms placed early and given the most direct sets. One leg movement per day. No squat rack needed. About 55 to 65 minutes per session.

Read this first
  • Schedule: Mon / Wed / Fri or any three non-consecutive days. Do A, B, C in order.
  • Order: each day runs First (one press, one pull) → Arms (biceps and triceps, paired) → Then (one more compound) → Last (delts, flyes, legs). Arms come early because what you train early grows a little more. Within a group, any order is fine. Keep each exercise in the same part of the session week to week so your log stays comparable.
  • If a machine is taken: do the other exercise in that group and come back, or use the substitute. Every exercise has two video links: the blue one is the main exercise, the grey one is its substitute. Same movement pattern and rep range is what counts. Only ever skip from the Last group. Never skip a press, a pull, or an arm exercise.
  • Effort on compounds: weeks 1 to 3 stop 3 to 4 reps short of failure while you learn form. From week 4, end every set 1 to 2 reps short of failure.
  • Effort on arms: every set 0 to 2 reps short of failure, and the last set of each arm exercise goes to actual failure. Arms tolerate this well and grow better for it.
  • Progression: when you hit the top of the rep range on every set, add the smallest weight increment next session and build back up. Until then, beat last time on at least one set. On arm exercises use the smallest jump you can find: add-on plates, half-kilo dumbbell steps, or one pin on the stack. A 5 kg jump on a curl is too big.
  • Rest: 2 min on presses, rows, pulldowns, and leg movements. Arms are paired: one set of curls, 45 sec, one set of triceps, 45 sec, repeat. 60 sec on delts and flyes.
  • Warm-up: 5 min easy skipping at home. At the gym, 2 light ramp-up sets on the first exercise of the day and 1 on the next press or pull. Ramp sets are half to three-quarters of working weight, easy, and do not count. Arms need no warm-up after the press and pull.
  • Tempo: full range of motion. Lower under control for 2 to 3 seconds. Feel the stretch at the bottom of presses, curls, and extensions.
  • Deload: every 6 to 8 weeks, or when most lifts go backwards for two weeks, do one week at half the sets with the same weights.
Outside the gym (this is half the result)
  • Protein: 1.6 to 2.2 g per kg of bodyweight per day, spread over 3 to 4 meals.
  • Calories: small surplus. Aim to gain 0.25 to 0.5% of bodyweight per week. Weigh in a few mornings a week and use the average.
  • Creatine monohydrate: 3 to 5 g every day, any time. The only supplement worth buying.
  • Sleep: 7 to 9 hours. Keep bed and wake times consistent.
  • Alcohol: keep it light and away from training nights.

What the groups mean

GroupWhat is in itRule
FirstOne press, one pullThe two biggest lifts, done fresh. Also most of your indirect arm volume. Substitute if a machine is taken, never skip.
ArmsOne biceps and one triceps exercise, 4 sets eachThe priority. Early in the session, paired to save time, last set to failure. Never skip.
ThenOne more compound: row or shoulder pressRounds out chest and back volume. Substitute rather than skip.
LastSide or rear delts, flyes, the leg movementNice to have. If you are out of time or the machine is busy, this is where you cut. Try not to skip legs more than 1 session in 3.

Tracking

Write down weight × reps for every set. Example: chest press 50 kg, 10 / 9 / 8. Next session, aim to beat at least one of those numbers. When all sets hit the top of the range, go up in weight. A tired day with lower numbers is noise, not a stall. Most lifts flat for two weeks means check food and sleep before changing the training.

For arms, also measure once a month: flexed upper arm, same spot, same time of day, same arm. Judge by the trend over 3 months, not week to week. A good first year adds 2 to 3 cm.
